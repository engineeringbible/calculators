# Asphalt calculator: tons of hot mix

**Summary:** Tons of hot-mix asphalt for a driveway, parking area or patch from area and compacted thickness, with density, waste allowance and cost per ton.

## Introduction

How much asphalt do you need? Multiply the area by the compacted thickness to get the volume, then multiply by the weight of compacted asphalt, about 145 lb per cubic foot. A driveway 12 ft wide and 50 ft long, paved 3 in thick, needs 10.9 short tons of hot mix, or about 11.4 tons with a little waste.

This calculator gives the compacted volume, the tonnage to order in short tons and metric tonnes, the spread rate in pounds per square yard per inch, and the cost at your price per ton. It is for homeowners pricing a driveway, and for contractors and estimators checking a quote or a patching job.

## Formulas

Volume and weight:

    V = L × W × t
    m = V × ρ
    m_order = m × (1 + waste)

Spread rate and the US rule of thumb:

    spread [lb/yd²/in] = ρ [lb/ft³] × 9 / 12
    tons = yd² × inches × 110 / 2 000

- L, W — length and width of the paved area; t — compacted thickness
- ρ — compacted density of the mix in place, 145 lb/ft³ (2.32 t/m³) by default
- waste — allowance for edges, an uneven base and material left in the equipment
- 1 short ton = 2 000 lb; 1 metric tonne = 1 000 kg = 2 205 lb

The rule of 110 lb per square yard per inch matches a density of 146.7 lb/ft³. At 145 lb/ft³ the spread rate is 108.75 lb per yd² per inch.

## Assumptions and limitations

- The area is a rectangle, or an area you enter, paved to an even thickness. Add the pieces of an irregular area.
- The density is that of the compacted mix. Dense-graded mixes weigh 144 to 160 lb/ft³. Ask the plant for the density of its mix when you need an exact figure.
- The thickness is the finished, compacted thickness. The paver lays a thicker loose mat, and the rollers compact it down.
- The calculation covers the asphalt only. The aggregate base below it, tack coat and sealer are separate items.

## Worked example

A driveway is 12 ft wide and 50 ft long, paved with 3 in of compacted asphalt at 145 lb/ft³. How many tons are needed with 5 % waste, and what do they cost at $100 per ton?

1. Area: 12 × 50 = 600 ft², or 66.7 yd².
2. Volume: 600 × 3 / 12 = 150 ft³, which is 5.56 yd³.
3. Weight: 150 × 145 = 21 750 lb, which is 10.88 short ton.
4. With 5 % waste: 10.88 × 1.05 = 11.42 short ton.
5. Cost: 11.42 × $100 = $1 142.

The rule of thumb gives nearly the same answer: 66.7 yd² × 3 in × 110 lb / 2 000 = 11.0 tons before waste. In metric tonnes the order is 10.4 t.

## FAQ

**How much does a ton of asphalt cover?**
At 145 lb/ft³, one short ton covers about 18.4 yd² at 1 in thick, 9.2 yd² at 2 in and 6.1 yd² at 3 in.

**How much does a cubic yard of asphalt weigh?**
About 3 915 lb, or 1.96 short tons, at 145 lb/ft³ compacted. In metric units a cubic metre weighs about 2.32 tonnes.

**How many tons of asphalt for a 20 × 20 ft area?**
At 2 in compacted, 66.7 ft³ × 145 lb/ft³ = 9 667 lb, or 4.83 short tons before waste.

**Where does the 110 lb per square yard per inch rule come from?**
A square yard 1 in thick is 0.75 ft³. At about 147 lb/ft³ that weighs 110 lb, a round figure used for estimating.

**How much waste should I allow?**
5 to 10 % is usual. Use more on small patches, uneven bases and jobs with many edges.

**Is asphalt sold in short tons or tonnes?**
US plants sell by the short ton of 2 000 lb. Metric countries sell by the tonne of 1 000 kg, which is 10 % heavier.
