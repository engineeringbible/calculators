Gravel calculator: cubic yards and tons

Gravel, crushed stone, sand or topsoil for an area in cubic yards, m³ and tons, with a compaction allowance, truck loads and cost.

Introduction

How much gravel do you need? Multiply the length, width and depth to get the volume, convert it to cubic yards, then multiply by the weight of a cubic yard to get tons. A path 20 ft long and 10 ft wide, covered 4 in deep, takes 2.47 cubic yards of crushed stone. With an allowance for compaction that is about 3.7 short tons.

This calculator does the sums for gravel, crushed stone, sand, sand and gravel, and topsoil. It gives the volume in place and the volume to order, the weight in short tons and metric tonnes, the number of truck loads and the cost. It is for homeowners, landscapers and contractors ordering bulk material by the yard or by the ton.

Formulas

Volume in place and volume to order:

V = L × W × d
V_order = V × (1 + c)

Weight, truck loads and cost:

m = V_order × ρ
loads = larger of V_order / truck volume and m / truck payload, rounded up
cost = tons × price per ton
  • L, W — length and width of the area; d — compacted depth, with inches divided by 12 to get feet
  • c — compaction and settlement allowance, as a fraction
  • ρ — loose bulk density of the delivered material
  • 1 yd³ = 27 ft³ = 0.7646 m³; 1 short ton = 2 000 lb = 907.2 kg

Assumptions and limitations

  • The area is a rectangle, or an area you enter, covered to an even depth. Split an irregular area into rectangles and add the results.
  • The depth is the finished depth after compaction. The allowance adds the volume lost when loose stone is spread and rolled. 10 to 15 % is usual for a compacted base.
  • The weights are typical loose weights. Wet material is heavier, and different rock types differ. Ask your quarry for its weight per yard when you buy by the ton.
  • Truck capacity and payload vary with the truck and with state weight limits. Enter your hauler's figures.

Worked example

A garden path is 20 ft long, 10 ft wide and 4 in deep, in crushed stone. How much should you order with a 10 % compaction allowance, and what does it cost at $40 per ton?

  1. Depth in feet: 4 / 12 = 0.333 ft.
  2. Volume: 20 × 10 × 0.333 = 66.7 ft³, which is 66.7 / 27 = 2.47 yd³.
  3. With 10 % allowance: 2.47 × 1.10 = 2.72 yd³, or 73.3 ft³.
  4. Weight: 2.72 yd³ × 2 697 lb/yd³ = 7 325 lb, which is 3.66 short ton.
  5. Cost: 3.66 × $40 = $146.50.

The load fits in one truck. In metric terms it is 2.08 m³ and 3.32 tonnes.

FAQ

How many tons are in a cubic yard of gravel?

About 1.35 short tons of crushed stone and 1.43 of dry gravel, loose. Sand weighs about 1.2 tons per yard and topsoil about 0.8.

How many square feet does a cubic yard of gravel cover?

A cubic yard is 27 ft³. It covers 162 ft² at 2 in deep, 108 ft² at 3 in and 81 ft² at 4 in.

How many cubic yards are in a ton of crushed stone?

About 0.74 yd³, since a loose cubic yard weighs about 2 700 lb.

Why add a compaction allowance?

Loose stone settles when it is spread and rolled. Ordering the compacted volume alone leaves the area short, so 10 to 15 % is usually added for a base course.

Why is the weight in place higher than the loose weight?

Compaction squeezes out voids. Caterpillar lists crushed stone at 1 600 kg/m³ loose but 2 670 kg/m³ in the solid bank.

How many bags of gravel make a cubic yard?

54 bags of 0.5 ft³, because a cubic yard is 27 ft³. Bulk delivery is usually cheaper above a cubic yard or two.
